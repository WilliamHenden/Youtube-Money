# AI Tools That Make the Videos For You: Research & Recommendations

*Researched November 2026. Prices change constantly and many review sites are affiliates or competitors, so check each vendor's pricing page and use free trials before paying annually.*

**Your constraints:** no editing experience, you don't want to use your own voice, and you want tools to do as much as possible.
**The channel we're building:** long-form (12–18 min) faceless business documentaries (see `01-business-strategy.md`).

---

## 1. The honest headline

**Yes, you can now make a complete documentary-style video without editing anything yourself.** Several tools take a topic or script and return a finished, narrated, subtitled video with footage and music.

There's a catch you need to understand before choosing:

> The more a tool does on its own, the more your videos look like everyone else's who uses that tool. Same stock clips, same voices, same pacing. That templated look is exactly what YouTube's *inauthentic content* policy targets (see strategy doc, Part 5).

So the goal isn't "one button, done." It's this:
**AI does all the *labour* (editing, voice, visuals, captions, assembly). You keep a light grip on the parts that make a video original: the topic, the angle and the facts.** Those take reading and decision-making, not editing skill. I can help with all three.

There are three realistic ways to do this, compared in §4.

## 2. The tools you asked about

### Higgsfield AI: **not the right core tool for this channel**
- **What it is:** a creative suite built on top of AI video and image models (it uses models from several companies). It's known for 70+ cinematic camera-move presets, visual effects, face swap, "AI influencer" tools and sound generation.
- **Price:** about $15 / $49 / $129 per month, credit-based. Reviewers warn that every retry costs credits and the bill adds up quickly.
- **Good at:** eye-catching 5–10 second cinematic clips, ads and social hooks.
- **Bad at:** anything long. It doesn't assemble a 15-minute narrated documentary.
- **Verdict:** at most an occasional source of a dramatic opening shot. **Don't subscribe at the start.**

### Abacus.AI (ChatLLM): **cheap all-rounder, but not a video maker**
- **What it is:** a $10–$20/month bundle that gives you many AI chat models in one place, plus "DeepAgent" (an autonomous task agent) and "Abacus Studio", which offers access to video models such as Veo 3.1, Kling 3 and Seedance 2.0.
- **Video reality:** Abacus Studio makes **short clips**, metered by credits (video eats credits fastest). Reviewers note it isn't built as a creative suite, so you'd still need separate voice and editing tools.
- **Verdict:** good value if you want cheap access to lots of models, but **it doesn't replace a video production tool**, and we already plan to use Claude for scripts. **Skip.**

**Shorts-only autopilots** (AutoShorts, Faceless.video, Crayo, Revid, from about $19–$40/mo): these generate *and auto-post* Shorts on a schedule. **Avoid them as a main channel.** It's the exact "mass-produced" model YouTube is demonetising, and Shorts pay pennies (see strategy doc). We'll make Shorts by clipping our long-form videos instead (Opus Clip, below).

**A trick worth knowing for "I don't have a good voice":** instead of a stock voice everyone else uses, you can:
1. **Design a unique voice** in ElevenLabs' Voice Design (describe it: "warm British male, 40s, measured documentary tone"). No one else has exactly that voice. **Recommended.**
2. Or **clone your own voice** once from 30+ minutes of recordings, then just type scripts. It's *your* voice with perfect delivery and no retakes. That's the strongest originality signal, since it's genuinely you, and you never record again.

Either way, **pick one voice and stick to it.** A consistent, recognisable narrator is part of the brand.

### 3.3 AI video and image generators (for B-roll shots, not whole videos)

The current quality leaders (Arena blind-vote leaderboard, 8 Nov 2026): **Google's Gemini Omni models**, **ByteDance Seedance 2.x**, **MiniMax H3** and **Kling 3**, with **Veo 3.1** and **Runway Gen-4.5** close behind. Rankings change monthly.

For a business documentary, use these **sparingly** (0–60 seconds per video) for shots no stock library has (e.g. "1970s Toys R Us store interior"). Real footage, photos, charts and maps are more credible. If an AI clip looks realistic, tick YouTube's "altered or synthetic content" box.
- **Easiest access:** Google AI Pro (~£19/mo) includes the **Flow** app with Veo and **Nano Banana** image generation. Or use the generators built into InVideo or VidRush.
- **Images for thumbnails and illustrations:** Nano Banana (Google), Midjourney, Ideogram (best with text).

### 3.5 Scripts, research and packaging

- **Claude (me):** research, outlines, scripts, fact-check tables, titles, descriptions, chapters. This is where your originality comes from, so it's the one thing I wouldn't hand to the generic writer inside a video tool.
- **Subscribr:** a YouTube-specific AI scriptwriter that studies your niche's top videos. Pricing has jumped (reports range from about $20 to $99+/mo since its August 2026 relaunch). **Not needed** if you're using Claude.
- **Thumbnails:** **Pikzels** is the most-used dedicated YouTube thumbnail AI (mixed Trustpilot reviews: watch out for annual billing with no refunds). Alternatives: Thumbsmith, ThumbnailGo, or an image model + a Canva template. Then use YouTube's free **Test & Compare** to A/B test.
- **Research and outliers:** vidIQ, 1of10 or OutlierKit (pick one), Google Trends (free).

### 3.6 Automation glue (connecting it all so it runs itself)

- **n8n** (self-host free / cloud ~£20/mo) or **Make**: visual "if this then that" workflows. There are ready-made templates (e.g. "ChatGPT script → ElevenLabs voice → Creatomate render → auto-upload to YouTube"). Most published templates are **Shorts-focused**, and many paid Gumroad ones are unvetted.
- **Creatomate / Shotstack / JSON2Video:** "render a video from a template file" services (~$40–$55/mo) that n8n workflows plug into.
- **Remotion:** code-based video rendering (free for individuals). This is how I'd build a fully custom pipeline in this repo.
- **Open-source "YouTube agents"** on GitHub (e.g. *youtube-automation-agent*, *YAAS*) chain research → script → voice → visuals → render → publish, with an approval step. Promising but young. Useful as inspiration; I'd rather build ours properly.
- **Auto-upload caveat:** uploads via YouTube's API are **forced to private until the Google project passes an audit**. Uploading manually in YouTube Studio takes 5 minutes and is fine at 1–2 videos a week.

## 4. Three ways to run production, and my recommendation

### Option 1: "Off-the-shelf autopilot" (fastest start)
**Claude (script) → VidRush or InVideo AI (makes the whole video, with ElevenLabs voice) → Pikzels/Canva (thumbnail) → upload in YouTube Studio**
- **Your time:** about 2–3 hours per video (choose the topic, read and approve the script, watch the result, regenerate bad scenes, pick a thumbnail)
- **Cost:** about £90–£180/month (Claude Pro £18 + VidRush or InVideo £25–£80 + ElevenLabs £17 + thumbnail tool £10–£30), plus per-video credits on VidRush
- **Pros:** you could publish the first video this week, with zero editing skills
- **Cons:** generic stock-footage look shared with thousands of channels, which is a monetisation risk; credit costs climb; you depend entirely on one vendor

### Option 2: "Hybrid" ✅ (recommended to start)
Same as Option 1, but with three upgrades that make your videos distinctive:
1. **Scripts written with me** using a proper research and fact-check process, never the tool's built-in writer.
2. **Your own designed ElevenLabs voice**, uploaded to the video tool, rather than its stock voices.
3. **Your own visual signature:** 3–6 custom charts per video showing the key numbers (I can generate these for you as image or video files) dropped into the video tool, plus a consistent thumbnail template.
Then **Descript** for any fixes (text-based editing) and **Opus Clip** for Shorts.
- **Your time:** about 3–5 hours per video
- **Cost:** about £120–£220/month
- **Why:** you can start in days with no editing skills, and the result is original enough to have a good chance of passing YouTube's monetisation review.

### Option 3: "Custom pipeline, built for you in this repo" (where we should end up)
I build a pipeline here that you run (or that runs on a schedule):
**topic → research pack → script (you approve) → ElevenLabs narration → stock footage + archive photos + code-generated charts and maps → automatic edit & render (Remotion) → captions → thumbnail options → title, description and chapters → ready to upload**
- **Your time:** about 1–2 hours per video (approving the topic, script and thumbnail; watching the final cut)
- **Cost:** lowest per video, about £5–£15 (API usage) plus ~£60–£100/month in subscriptions (ElevenLabs, a stock library, Claude)
- **Pros:** a unique look nobody else has, full control, cheapest at scale, and it can run several channels and languages. It's a business asset you own, not a vendor subscription.
- **Cons:** takes a few weeks of building and tuning; you'll need to create accounts and API keys (I'll guide you step by step).

### My recommendation
1. **This week:** start **Option 2**. Take free trials of **InVideo AI and TubeGen**, request **VidRush** access, and use **ElevenLabs Creator**. Make the same pilot video in each tool and keep the best one. Total spend is under £60.
2. **Meanwhile, I build Option 3** in this repo, starting with the parts that make the biggest quality difference: the research/script/fact-check engine and the chart generator. Those plug straight into Option 2 immediately.
3. **Switch to Option 3 for assembly** once its output beats the off-the-shelf tool in a side-by-side test.

### What to skip
❌ Higgsfield and Abacus.AI subscriptions · ❌ AI avatars (HeyGen avatars, etc.) · ❌ Shorts autopilots (AutoShorts, Crayo, Faceless.video, Revid) as the main channel · ❌ Hume (API reportedly shutting down) · ❌ Subscribr (redundant with Claude) · ❌ paid n8n templates from Gumroad · ❌ annual plans before you've tested a tool for a month

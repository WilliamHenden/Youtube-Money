# First Video: Topic Shortlist

*Researched 12 November 2026. View counts were read from YouTube search results that day. Facts in the "surprising answer" column are from memory and must be verified with sources at the script stage.*

## What the YouTube data showed

I searched eight candidate topics and looked at the top results for each.

1. **Proven topics have one or two big videos, often old.** Those show the audience exists.
2. **Almost every topic also has a cluster of brand-new, tiny channels** posting near-identical titles and getting between 0 and 100 views. "Why Google Pays Apple $20 Billion a Year" had six such videos in the past month, none above 2,000 views.
3. **So the topic alone won't carry a new channel.** The videos that fail are the generic ones. The first video needs a fresh angle, a strong title and thumbnail, and the story quality set out in `05-production-standards.md`.

## The shortlist

| # | Working title | Central question | Surprising answer (to verify) | Evidence of demand | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Why glasses cost so much** | Why does a bit of plastic and two lenses cost £300? | One company you've probably never heard of makes many of the famous brands, owns big high-street chains, and makes the lenses too. | CBS News 9.98M views, CNBC 745K. Both are 7–14 years old, and the story has moved on a lot since. | The often-quoted "80% of the market" figure is disputed, so the script must use numbers that hold up. |
| 2 | Why every zip says YKK | Why are the same three letters on almost everything you wear? | A Japanese company became the default by making its own machines, metal and thread, and by treating a 10p part as the thing that can ruin a £100 jacket. | WSJ 3.27M (3 months ago), a small channel at 286K (3 months ago). Currently hot. | A simple story that may be thin for 8 minutes. WSJ covered it very recently. |
| 3 | The airline that's really a bank | How do airlines make money when flying barely pays? | For big US airlines, selling loyalty points to credit card companies earns more than carrying passengers. | Wendover 7.36M, WSJ 1.38M. | A famous video already exists, the story is mostly American, and there's a cluster of new copycats. |
| 4 | Red Bull doesn't make Red Bull | How did a company that owns no factories become the biggest energy drink? | It outsources production and spends its money on marketing, sport and events. | Athletic Interest 9.82M, Wendover 1.77M (8 months ago). | Covered very recently by a major channel. |

Checked and dropped:
- **Rolex being owned by a charity:** the existing videos have a few thousand views at most, and several new tiny channels posted it this month.
- **Google paying Apple:** flooded with copycats, and the facts are changing with court cases.
- **Aldi's low prices:** proven demand (3.5M and 4.9M views), but new copycats are appearing, including one two days old.
- **LEGO's near-bankruptcy:** only Shorts have done well on it.

## Recommendation: #1, glasses

- **It passes the "I already knew that" test.** Most people have never heard of the company behind their glasses.
- **Everyone has a stake.** Most families include someone who pays for glasses, so friends and family are a fair test audience.
- **Proven demand with room for a new version.** The big videos are old, and there's plenty they couldn't cover: the 2018 merger that joined the biggest frame maker with the biggest lens maker, its move into UK high-street opticians, and online challengers.
- **A natural question → pause → payoff structure**, with several reveals to stack ("…and they own the shops. …and they make the lenses.").
- **It suits our visuals:** a wall of brand names collapsing into one owner, a price breakdown, a timeline of takeovers, a map of shops, real photos.
- **A useful takeaway:** how to pay less (you're entitled to your prescription; you can buy elsewhere). That fits the "what this teaches you" ending.
- **A UK angle** that American channels won't have.

To get right at the script stage: exact ownership and brand lists, market-share figures that are properly sourced, and fair wording about a real company (facts and attributed claims only).

# AI-Powered YouTube Business: Strategy Report

*Prepared November 2026. This is a working document: review the policy and pricing figures before you spend money.*

---

## 0. Executive summary (read this first)

**The short version**

1. **The £4,000/month for a 10k-subscriber channel figure is possible but not typical.** A channel getting 10–15k views per video and posting 1–2 videos a week usually earns **£200–£1,200/month from ads**. It reaches £4,000 only if it has (a) a high-value audience such as finance, software or business viewers in the US or UK, **and** (b) sponsorships and affiliate income on top of ads, **or** (c) a large back catalogue that earns far more views than the new uploads. Subscribers don't earn money. Views, watch time and the value of the audience do.
2. **The easy AI ideas are where people lose money.** AI nursery rhymes, "boring history for sleep", Reddit story narration, top-10 facts and Shorts-only channels are crowded and get low RPMs. YouTube is actively targeting them under its *inauthentic content* policy, which in January 2026 was reportedly used to terminate 16 channels with about 35M subscribers between them. Kids' content looks easy, but its RPM is roughly **a tenth** of education content.
3. **My recommendation: a faceless, long-form business documentary channel ("how companies really make money / why they failed").** It has proven demand (MagnatesMedia, ColdFusion, Modern MBA and many mid-sized channels), some of the highest RPMs available to a faceless channel (business and finance-adjacent), strong sponsorship demand (SaaS, fintech, investing apps, courses), and a format AI can do most of the work for (research → script → narration → charts, stock footage and motion graphics). It stays defensible because each video is an original researched argument, which is exactly what YouTube's policy rewards.
4. **Operating model: start with Model B (AI-assisted with human quality control).** Expect about 5–7 hours of your time per finished video once the system is running, £100–£180/month in software, and £5–£20 in direct costs per video. Fully automated "Model A" channels are the ones being demonetised.
5. **Realistic money:** at the realistic all-in yield of about **£7.50 per 1,000 long-form views** (ads, sponsors and affiliates combined), **£2,000/month needs about 270k monthly views** and **£4,000/month needs about 530k**. For a well-run channel in this niche that is plausibly **12–24 months away, not 90 days**, and some well-run channels never get there.
6. **The bigger business:** treat the channel as the first output of a reusable **documentary production engine**. The same pipeline can serve a second documentary niche, dubbed audio tracks (YouTube now supports multi-language audio for all creators), a newsletter, and potentially B2B explainer work for brands.

**Things I need from you that would change the recommendation** (see §9): your realistic weekly hours, your budget, whether you'd narrate yourself, and whether you (or I, in this repo) can maintain a code-based pipeline.

## PART 1: Channel concepts evaluated as businesses

### Key concepts first

- **CPM** = what advertisers pay per 1,000 ad impressions. **RPM** = what *you* receive per 1,000 views, across all views including those with no ad. RPM is typically about 40–55% of CPM. **Only RPM matters for your projections.**
- **Long-form**: creators keep 55% of ad revenue. **Shorts**: revenue is pooled and shared, and creators keep 45% of their allocation. In practice Shorts RPM is **pennies** (about $0.01–$0.15 per 1,000 engaged views in most 2026 estimates).
- RPM is driven by **(1) who watches** (US/UK/Germany/Nordics/Australia pay most), **(2) topic** (money, software, business attract high-value advertisers), **(3) video length** (8+ minutes allows mid-rolls, and very long videos carry many more ads), and **(4) season** (Q4 is high and January drops sharply).

### Notes on the important ones

**Children's content (#1–2): commercially worse than it looks.**
- Under the US COPPA rules (YouTube's 2019 FTC settlement), any video "made for kids" must be labelled that way. That label **disables personalised ads, comments, notifications, the mini-player, end screens and most community features**. The outcome is an RPM of roughly **£0.25–£1.25**, against £4–£10 for adult education content.
- Competition includes Cocomelon, Little Baby Bum and Super Simple Songs, which have enormous libraries and studio budgets, plus thousands of AI copycat channels.
- Pressure is rising. Bloomberg (Dec 2025) reported on AI "slop" aimed at babies, 200+ child-development experts petitioned Google in 2026 to stop serving AI videos to children, and YouTube reportedly removed some AI kids' channels from YPP and YouTube Kids. **This is the category most likely to face sudden new restrictions.**
- To earn £2,000/month at a £0.75 RPM you'd need about **2.7 million views a month**. That's possible for a hit, but it's a lottery, not a business plan.

**"Boring history for sleep" (#4): the cautionary tale.** Channels like "Boring History" reportedly earned very high RPMs (one digest claims about $30 RPM on 1.88M views in a month, unverified) because 2–3 hour videos carry dozens of mid-rolls. Within about a year the niche flooded (404 Media covered the "AI boring history" glut), and the original channel was reportedly **demonetised** in early 2026. Lesson: **a high-RPM format that AI can make with no human input attracts copycats until YouTube's enforcement catches up with it.** Avoid any format that a competitor could copy with the same prompt.

**Multi-language (#20–21): your "MrBeast" idea, updated for 2026.** MrBeast originally ran separate dubbed channels (his Spanish channel reportedly got up to 100M views per video). YouTube has since built this into the platform. **Multi-language audio tracks are now available to all creators, and YouTube auto-dubbing covers about 27 languages.** YouTube reported that creators using dubbed tracks got **15%+ (later reported as ~25%) of watch time from non-primary-language viewers**. So you don't need separate "group channels": you upload one video with several audio tracks, and the views, watch time and subscribers all count towards **one** channel. That's the better model, because separate channels re-uploading the same visuals risk being flagged as duplicate content. **Caveats:** Spanish, Portuguese and Hindi viewers have much lower RPMs, and auto-dub quality is uneven. Use it as a **free bonus layer** on top of an English channel. German, French and Japanese tracks are worth the most per view.

**Opportunities you probably wouldn't have considered**
- **German-language faceless documentaries.** Germany has high CPMs and far fewer faceless documentary channels than English. Produce the script in English, then create a professionally reviewed German narration. This is a second channel or audio track worth testing in phase 2.
- **Map/data-driven geography channels.** The visuals (maps, charts, timelines) can be **generated in code** from data rather than by AI image models. That makes it the most automatable *and* most original-looking format, since it's not a stock or AI aesthetic.
- **Buying a small monetised channel** (marketplaces such as Flippa list them; one 2025 listing was a sleep-history channel with $4.6k/month revenue). This skips the YPP wait, but it carries policy risk if the channel was built on templated content. Only consider it later, with due diligence.
- **Selling the engine.** Once your pipeline makes good documentaries, brands and B2B companies pay well for "company story" or explainer videos. Your channel becomes the portfolio (see Part 8).

### Why business documentaries win

3. **Sponsorships come naturally.** Business audiences match the biggest YouTube sponsors (SaaS, investing apps, VPNs, online courses, newsletters, website builders). Sponsorship CPMs for business and finance content are typically **$20–$50 per 1,000 views** (estimate).
4. **Most of the work suits AI, but not all of it, and that's a good thing.** AI handles research gathering, first drafts, narration, charts, thumbnail drafts and metadata well. The **angle and the fact-check** need a human. That ~20% human layer is precisely what keeps you on the right side of the inauthentic content policy, and it's what your competitors who automate everything skip.
5. **Defensibility.** A well-researched, well-argued script on "Why Superdry collapsed" can't be copied with a single prompt. Each video is evergreen and earns from search for years.
6. **Scalability.** The topic supply is effectively unlimited (every company, industry and business scandal). The pipeline also transfers directly to history, geography and science documentaries.

### Why not the others first
- **Tutorials:** the highest revenue per view, but they need real screen recordings, they go out of date quickly as software changes, and they're harder to automate. Good as channel #2.
- **Geography:** the most automatable, but lower RPM. A strong candidate for channel #2 using the same engine.
- **History:** a crowded field with AI-slop collateral damage (YouTube has demonetised even legitimate history channels), and lower RPM than business.
- **UK finance:** see the regulatory note above.

## PART 3: The actual money

### 3.1 Subscribers vs views vs monthly views

- **Subscribers** are a lagging vanity number. Many subscribers never see a new upload, because YouTube decides distribution by how *each video* performs with *any* viewer. Sponsors and AdSense pay on **views and watch time**, not subscribers.
- **Views per video** shows how well your new uploads perform.
- **Total monthly views** is what you get paid on. It equals *new video views + back catalogue views*. For evergreen documentaries the back catalogue often becomes **50–80% of monthly views** after a year.
- **Watch hours** decide YPP eligibility: 4,000 hours = 240,000 minutes. At a 6-minute average view duration, that's about **40,000 long-form views** in 12 months.

### 3.5 Your £4,000 assumption, tested

Your example: 10k subscribers, 10–15k views per video, 1–2 videos per week.

- New uploads per month: 4–9 videos → **40k–135k views/month** from new videos.
- Back catalogue: for a channel with 50–100 evergreen videos, perhaps another 20k–150k.
- **Total: about 60k–285k monthly views.**
- Ads at a typical £3–£6 RPM: **£180–£1,700/month.**

**For £4,000/month to be true, at least one of these has to hold:**
1. **The niche has finance/B2B-level RPMs (£15–£25)** with a US/UK audience. At 200k views × £20 RPM = £4,000. Real, but only in the highest-paying niches.
2. **Sponsors on most videos.** 8 videos × 12k views × a £25–£35 sponsor CPM ≈ £2,400–£3,400/month, plus ads (~£800). This is the **most common way small business, finance and tech channels earn £4k+.**
3. **Very long videos** (2–3 hours) with many mid-rolls inflate the RPM. This is the "sleep content" mechanic, which is now high-risk.
4. **The back catalogue is much bigger than it looks:** a channel getting 10–15k views on *new* videos can be getting 500k+ monthly views from old ones.
5. **Off-YouTube income** (a course, consulting, a product) gets credited to "YouTube" in the anecdote.

**Verdict:** £4,000/month at that size is **achievable in a high-value niche with active sponsorship sales**, and **unlikely from AdSense alone**. The typical figure for that channel profile is **£300–£1,500/month**. That's why I chose a niche where sponsorship is a realistic second income stream.

## PART 4: AI tools & automation architecture

### 4.1 The pipeline and how much a human must do at each stage

```
[1 Topic discovery] → [2 Research pack] → [3 Script] → [4 Fact-check] → [5 Narration]
      → [6 Visual plan + assets] → [7 Assembly/render] → [8 Captions+SFX/music]
      → [9 Thumbnail+title] → [10 Metadata] → [11 Upload/schedule] → [12 Analytics → back to 1]
```

| Stage | Automation level | Your role |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Topic discovery | **Mostly automated**: scrape competitor outliers and score topics | Pick 1 from a ranked shortlist of 10 (5 min) |
| 2. Research pack | **Automated** (Claude with web search; collects sources and numbers) | Skim |
| 3. Script | **AI draft** | **The key human step**: the angle, the hook, cutting boring parts (45–60 min) |
| 4. Fact-check | **AI-assisted**: a second model pass cross-checks every claim against sources | **Human approval required** for every number and claim (30–45 min) |
| 5. Narration | **Fully automated** (ElevenLabs), or your own voice | Listen to the first 60 seconds and spot-check |
| 6. Visuals | **Mostly automated**: charts and maps from code; stock and archive search by keyword; AI images and video where nothing exists | Review the shot list; swap weak shots (30–45 min) |
| 7. Assembly | **Automated** (Remotion / Creatomate / Shotstack template from a timeline file) | Watch the full render once (20 min) |
| 8. Captions, SFX, music | **Automated** (captions from the script; licensed music library) | n/a |
| 9. Thumbnail and title | **AI generates 3–5 options** | **Human picks**; then run YouTube's built-in Test & Compare A/B test (15–30 min) |
| 10. Description, chapters, tags | **Fully automated** | n/a |
| 11. Upload and schedule | **Automatable** via the YouTube Data API (needs a Google API audit, otherwise uploads are forced private), or 5 minutes in YouTube Studio | Click publish |
| 12. Analytics and feedback | **Fully automated weekly report** (YouTube Analytics API → Claude) | Read the report; agree the next week's changes (20 min) |

**Steps that must stay human: the angle (3), the truth (4), and the packaging (9).** Those three decide almost all of your views and your monetisation approval.

## PART 5: Monetisation policy (read before producing anything)

> I couldn't fetch YouTube's Help Centre pages from this cloud session (support.google.com is blocked by its network settings). The summary below comes from consistent secondary reporting. **Check it against the official pages before you launch:** "YouTube Partner Program overview & eligibility", "YouTube channel monetisation policies" (inauthentic content and reused content sections), "Disclosing use of altered or synthetic content", and "Determining if your content is made for kids".

### 5.1 Eligibility (as widely reported for 2026)
- **Ads tier:** 1,000 subscribers **and** either 4,000 public long-form watch hours in the last 12 months **or** 10M public Shorts views in the last 90 days.
- **Lower tier** (fan funding and Shopping, no ads): 500 subscribers, 3 public uploads in 90 days, and 3,000 watch hours or 3M Shorts views.
- One source reports the ads-tier thresholds may change for applications from **1 February 2027**. This is unverified, so check it.
- You also need 2-step verification, an AdSense account, no active Community Guidelines strikes, and you must live in an eligible country (the UK is eligible).
- **Monetisation can be removed later** if the channel stops complying or goes inactive for about 6 months.

### 5.2 The policies that kill AI channels

| Policy | What it targets | How our system avoids it |
|---|---|---|
| **Inauthentic content** (renamed from "repetitious content" on 15 July 2025) | Mass-produced or templated content, near-identical videos, "slideshows with TTS", content that differs only cosmetically | Each video is an **original argument with a researched narrative**; varied structure; a distinct visual language; sensible cadence (1–3 per week, **not** daily) |
| **Reused content** | Others' content without significant original commentary or education: compilations, re-uploads, read-aloud Reddit posts | Original scripts; licensed stock; short, transformative use of news or press clips only with commentary |
| **Altered or synthetic content disclosure** | Realistic AI depictions of real people, places or events that viewers could mistake for real | Tick the disclosure when AI generates realistic scenes (e.g. an AI-made "factory floor" shot). Charts, stylised illustrations and AI narration of your own script generally don't need it. **YouTube says the label itself doesn't reduce reach or monetisation**; *not* disclosing is the risk. |
| **Made for Kids** (COPPA) | Child-directed content: limited ads, no comments | We're not making kids' content. Set the channel to "not made for kids". |
| **Advertiser-friendly guidelines** | Violence, tragedy, controversial topics → limited or no ads | Business collapses are fine. Handle fraud and deaths factually, without sensational thumbnails. |
| **Copyright** | Music, footage, other people's videos | Licensed libraries only; keep licence records; use company logos only editorially |
| **Spam / deceptive practices** | Misleading titles or thumbnails, keyword stuffing | Thumbnails must reflect the content; honest titles |

**AI narration is NOT banned.** YouTube's position, as reported, is that AI tools are allowed when the creator adds **significant original value**. The risk is a channel that *looks like a template*.

### 5.3 What a YPP reviewer sees, and how to pass

Reviewers reportedly look at your channel's **main theme, most-viewed videos, newest videos, the videos with the most watch time, metadata and About section**. So:

1. **Every video in the first 20–30 must be good.** Don't publish weak or test videos publicly; keep them unlisted.
2. **Make a visible human editorial identity.** A named channel persona or editor (it can be faceless), an About section that explains the editorial process ("researched and written by…, sources in every description"), and a consistent, distinctive narrator voice.
3. **Cite sources** in every description. This signals research, not slop.
4. **Vary the structure.** Don't use the same intro line, the same 7-section template and the same thumbnail layout every time. Keep consistent *branding*, not identical *formats*.
5. **Keep a sensible cadence.** 1–2 high-effort videos a week beats 14 low-effort ones and doesn't look like a content farm.
6. **Keep the channel to one theme.** Mixed topics confuse both the algorithm and the reviewers.
7. **Show originality signals:** original charts and maps, commentary and opinion ("here's why I think they failed"), and human judgement.
8. **Keep production records** (scripts, sources, licences) in case you need to appeal. If you're rejected, you can typically reapply after 30 days. Fix the underlying issue first.

**No one can guarantee monetisation.** These are the factors you control, and the system is designed around them.

### Which model to start with: **B, built to grow into C**

- Model A optimises the wrong thing. On YouTube the scarce resource is **viewer attention and YouTube's trust**, not production capacity. A fully automated channel produces lots of videos nobody watches and that YPP reviewers reject.
- Model B puts your limited hours into the three stages that decide the outcome (angle, truth, packaging), and automates everything else.
- Every repetitive step in Model B gets written down (prompts, templates, checklists) as you go. That documentation *is* Model C: once a video reliably takes 5 hours, you can hand the script-editing hours to a freelancer and start channel #2.

## PART 7: 90-day launch plan

### The channel

- **Concept:** faceless business documentaries answering *"How does this company really make money?"* and *"Why did this company actually fail?"*, explained with clear numbers, original charts and a strong narrative.
- **Audience:** English-speaking adults aged 22–45 in the US, UK, Canada and Australia who are curious about business, money and brands. Interests: entrepreneurship, investing, careers.
- **Positioning hook:** *the money mechanics.* Every video has one surprising number at its core (e.g. "Costco makes most of its profit from a £60 card, not groceries").
- **Your UK edge:** mix global brands with British stories that have global appeal (Greggs, Ryanair, Wetherspoons, Superdry, Woolworths, Rolls-Royce, Dyson). Fewer channels cover these, and they bring UK viewers with strong RPMs.
- **Format:** 12–18 minutes (allows mid-rolls without padding), cold-open hook in the first 15 seconds, 4–6 chapters, original charts every 30–60 seconds.
- **Cadence:** 1 long-form video/week for weeks 1–6, rising to 2/week if quality holds. 3–5 Shorts/week, clipped from the long-form videos.
- **Name:** short, brandable, not "AI"-sounding. We can brainstorm and check name and handle availability together.

### Week-by-week

**Weeks 1–2: Validate and build the foundation**
- Competitor map: 25–40 channels in business docs. Use the YouTube API to pull every video, then compute an **outlier score** (video views ÷ channel median). Expect 100–200 outlier videos. *(I can script this.)*
- Pattern analysis (with Claude): which **topic types, title structures and thumbnail styles** over-perform; which companies have been covered too often; where the gaps are.
- Build a **topic bank of 50 ideas**, each scored for demand (outliers/search), freshness (not covered in 12 months), story strength (conflict, a twist, a number) and sponsor fit.
- Channel setup: Google account, brand account, 2FA, handle, banner, About section (editorial statement), "not made for kids", default description template with sources, AdSense eligibility prep.
- Tools: Claude, ElevenLabs (choose or design ONE voice, or record yours), Storyblocks/Envato, Epidemic Sound, Canva/Figma, and this repo's pipeline skeleton.
- **Style guide:** narration tone, chart colours and fonts, thumbnail template families (2–3, so they don't look identical), music moods.

**Weeks 3–4: Pilot videos (private)**
- Produce **videos 1–2 end-to-end**, timing every stage. Expect 12–20 hours each the first time.
- Watch them as a stranger would: where would you click away? Fix the template.
- Write the first **script prompt pack** and **fact-check checklist** based on what went wrong.
- Publish video 1 at the end of week 4 only if it's genuinely good.

**Weeks 5–8: Ship the first 10 videos**
- Publish 1/week (2/week if you're ahead), plus Shorts clipped from each video.
- **How to make the first 10:** pick from the topic bank, mixing:
  - 4 "Why did X fail?" (e.g. Superdry, Toys R Us, Woolworths UK, Blockbuster from an unusual angle)
  - 3 "How does X actually make money?" (e.g. Costco, Ryanair, Wetherspoons)
  - 2 "The hidden business of…" (e.g. Rolex's supply control, the economics of meal-deal pricing)
  - 1 timely story tied to current business news (higher early views, shorter shelf life)
  - *These are illustrative. Final choices should come from the outlier data.*
- Every video: 3 title options and 3 thumbnails generated → you pick → run **Test & Compare**.
- By the end of week 8, automate stages 2, 5, 7, 8 and 10.

**Weeks 9–12: Optimise from data and automate further**
- Weekly analytics report (automated): CTR, retention curve, average view duration, traffic sources, which videos drive subscribers.
- Double down on the best-performing topic type; drop the worst.
- Re-package (new title and thumbnail) any video whose CTR is below 3% after 2 weeks.
- Automate stages 1 (topic scoring), 6 (charts from data) and 12 (the analytics loop).
- Turn on YouTube auto-dubbing and check the quality of the Spanish, German and French tracks.
- Start a simple **email list** (free newsletter: "one business story a week", built from your scripts).

### Metrics and targets (benchmarks for a new channel; these are estimates)

| Metric | Healthy for a new documentary channel | Action if below |
|---|---|---|
| Impressions CTR | 4–8% | Rework thumbnails and titles first. This is usually the bottleneck. |
| Viewers still watching at 30 sec | ≥65–70% | Rewrite the hook; cut slow intros |
| Average % viewed (12–18 min video) | 35–50% | Tighten the scripts; more visual changes; cut tangents |
| Views per video, first 30 days | Videos 1–10: 100–2,000 is normal. Videos 10–30: aim for a few at 5k+ | See the decision rules below |
| Subscribers per 1,000 views | 5–15 | Stronger end-screen and series structure |
| Returning viewers | Growing month over month | Build series ("Failed Brands", "Money Mechanics") |
| Hours per video (your time) | Down from ~15 → 6 by video 10 | Automate the slowest stage |

### Realistic milestones by day 90
- 10–14 long-form videos and 30–50 Shorts published.
- A repeatable pipeline: **≤6 human hours per video**.
- A clear read on which topic type and packaging works.
- Most likely: **200–1,500 subscribers and 500–3,000 watch hours.** YPP by day 90 is possible but **not expected**. Typical is months 4–9.
- One breakout video (50k+) is possible and would change everything. Don't plan on it.

### When to change strategy
- **After 10 videos:** if median views < 300 *and* CTR < 3%, packaging is the problem. Spend a sprint only on titles and thumbnails (including re-packaging old videos).
- **After 20 videos:** if *no* video has passed ~5k views despite decent CTR and retention, the **angle** is wrong. Pivot within business docs (e.g. to "failed brands" only, or a tech-companies-only focus).
- **After 30 videos (~month 6–7):** if still nothing works, switch the engine to the #2 or #3 niche (tutorials or geography). The pipeline carries over.

### When to launch a second channel
Only when **all** of these are true:
1. Channel 1 is in YPP or clearly on track (≥2,500 watch hours).
2. You produce a video in **≤5 hours of your time** with stable quality.
3. You have 20+ unused, validated topics and 4+ videos in the buffer.
Realistically that's **month 6–9**, not within the first 90 days. Best candidates: a **German-language version** of the business-docs channel, or a **geography/maps** channel using the same engine.

## PART 8: Thinking bigger than one channel

1. **YouTube as the engine for winning an audience; the business is the audience plus the production system.**
2. **Repurposing (low effort, automatable):**
   - Shorts, TikTok, Instagram Reels and Facebook Reels: 45–60 second "money fact" clips from each video. These monetise poorly but build reach.
   - **LinkedIn:** business case studies do unusually well there with professionals, which helps sponsorship and B2B credibility.
   - **Newsletter** (beehiiv or Substack): your scripts are already newsletter-ready. Business newsletters sell sponsorships at good rates and can grow a **paid tier** (e.g. a deeper weekly analysis).
   - **Podcast:** the narration audio, published as an audio show (Spotify video podcasts too).
3. **Affiliate:** business books (Amazon/Audible), SaaS tools you actually use, newsletter platforms. Be cautious with investing and trading-app affiliates in the UK (FCA financial promotion rules).
4. **Sponsorship sales:** once you have ~10k views per video, list on sponsor marketplaces (e.g. YouTube BrandConnect, Passionfroot) and pitch SaaS brands directly. Price on expected views, not subscribers.
5. **Digital products (later):** e.g. a "business model teardown" PDF pack, Notion templates for investors and founders, or a course on business analysis. These only make sense once you have trust and an email list.
6. **Replication:** the same engine (research → script → fact-check → narration → code-generated visuals) can power a portfolio of documentary channels: business, geography, history, science, plus language versions. Each new channel costs far less than the first.
7. **B2B services (the most scalable pound for pound):** companies pay £500–£3,000+ for professional explainer or "company story" videos. Your channel is the portfolio and the pipeline is your cost advantage. This turns a creator business into a **media production business**, which doesn't depend on YouTube's ad policy.
8. **Exit value:** monetised channels with clean histories and documented production systems are sellable assets (marketplaces like Flippa list them as a multiple of monthly profit). A templated AI channel is worth much less because buyers price in demonetisation risk.

**The most scalable long-term model:** a **multi-channel documentary studio**: one production engine, 2–4 high-RPM documentary channels (including at least one non-English), a newsletter, sponsorship sales across the network, and B2B commissioned work using the same pipeline.

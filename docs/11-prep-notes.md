## The intro and outro options

The standards already say: no logo before the hook, one subscribe moment under five seconds, and an ending that sells the next video. Every option keeps to that.

**Intro** (all come after the opening question and its pause)
- **A. Straight to the title.** What the sample does. Fastest, nothing to skip.
- **B. A three-second channel sting, then the title.** The mark lands, the name is spelled out with a short musical signature, and the mark travels to its corner. Builds the brand, costs three seconds at the most fragile point for retention.
- **C. The logo lands as the title appears.** No extra time: the mark starts large behind the title and settles into its corner, with the musical signature. A middle path.

My recommendation is **C**: it brands every video without adding a second before the story continues. B is the one to pick if the channel name matters more than the first-minute retention.

**Subscribe moment:** "If you're enjoying this, subscribing is free, and it really helps the channel." About four seconds. The owner could record this line in their own voice.

**Outro**
- **A. Straight to the next-video tease** over the end screen.
- **B. The lesson in one line first** ("Decide the rule before the money arrives"), then the tease.

My recommendation is **B** for this channel, because every video ends on a takeaway and the one-line version is what people remember and repeat.

The channel name and the line under it ("How money really works") are placeholders.

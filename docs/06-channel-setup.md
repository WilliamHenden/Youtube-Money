# YouTube Channel Setup, Upload Routine and Affiliate Links

*Researched 12 November 2026. Items marked **(official)** were read on YouTube's own help pages. The rest comes from creator guides and should be treated as good practice, not rules.*

## 1. One-time channel setup

### Account and security
- [ ] Use the spare Google account, with a strong password, 2-step verification and a recovery phone number. 2-step verification is required for monetisation later.
- [ ] Create the channel as its own brand channel, then add your main Google account as a second owner (YouTube Studio → Settings → Permissions).

### Unlock features straight away
- [ ] **Verify the channel with a phone number** (YouTube Studio → Settings → Channel → Feature eligibility → Intermediate features). This unlocks videos longer than 15 minutes and custom thumbnails. **(official)**
- [ ] **Advanced features** (for example pinned comments and higher daily upload limits) come from building channel history, or faster through ID or video verification where YouTube offers it. **(official)** Do the ID or video verification if it's offered.
- Some guides say clickable links in descriptions also need advanced features. I couldn't confirm that on YouTube's own page, so check the Feature eligibility panel once the channel exists.

### Channel settings (YouTube Studio → Settings)
- [ ] **Audience: "No, set this channel as not made for kids."** This matters: made-for-kids content loses personalised ads, comments and notifications.
- [ ] **Country of residence: United Kingdom.** It should be your real country; it's used for monetisation eligibility and payment.
- [ ] **Channel keywords:** 5–10 plain phrases describing the channel (for example: business explained, how companies make money, business stories).
- [ ] **Upload defaults:** set the standard description footer (sources line, subscribe line, any disclosures), category "Education", language English, and "not made for kids".
- [ ] **Comments:** leave on, with "hold potentially inappropriate comments for review".

### Branding (needs the channel name first)
- [ ] Profile picture: 800 × 800 px, readable as a tiny circle.
- [ ] Banner: 2560 × 1440 px, with everything important inside the central 1546 × 423 px (the only part visible on every device). It should say what the channel is and how often it posts.
- [ ] Video watermark: 150 × 150 px (acts as a subscribe button on videos).
- [ ] Channel description: the first two lines must say who it's for and what they'll get, because that's all that shows before "more".
- [ ] Handle: claim the same one on YouTube, Instagram, TikTok and Facebook.
- Claude can make the logo, banner, watermark and description in the channel's style once the name is chosen.

### Later
- [ ] A 30–45 second channel trailer for new visitors, once there are a few videos.
- [ ] Playlists by series, which encourage watching a second video.

## 2. Monetisation requirements (official)

- **Ads:** 1,000 subscribers, plus either 4,000 public watch hours in the last 12 months or 10 million public Shorts views in the last 90 days.
- **Shorts watch time does not count** towards the 4,000 hours. Long-form videos earn those.
- No active Community Guidelines strikes when you apply.
- A channel that hasn't uploaded or posted for six months can lose monetisation.
- YouTube's page says updated terms start on **1 February 2027** and must be accepted in YouTube Studio by 31 January 2027 to keep monetising. Read that notice when the channel is set up.

## 3. Upload routine for every video

The "upload it unlisted, wait, then publish" advice is real, and YouTube's own help page recommends it. The reason is practical, not a trick on the algorithm.

1. **Upload as private or unlisted** (or schedule it for later). **(official)**
2. **Wait for the checks to finish:** copyright and ad suitability. **(official)** Also wait until the HD version has finished processing, so the first viewers don't get a blurry copy. Guides suggest allowing a couple of hours.
3. Fill in while you wait: title, thumbnail, description with sources, chapters, end screen (next video + subscribe), and the "altered content" answer.
4. **Then publish**, or let the schedule do it. Publishing with checks complete means ads can run from the first view.

I found nothing official saying that the private or unlisted stage affects how a video is recommended.

Other points:
- **Titles and thumbnails must match the video.** Misleading ones break YouTube's rules.
- **Post at a consistent time.** Pick one slot and keep to it.
- **Don't delete and re-upload** a published video to "reset" it.

## 4. Affiliate links

### The rules
- **In the UK, an affiliate link counts as advertising.** The Advertising Standards Authority says commission-earning links must be clearly labelled, and a note buried at the bottom of a description is not enough. Label links plainly (for example "Ad: affiliate link, I earn a commission") and, if a product is recommended in the video itself, say so in the video.
- **YouTube's "includes paid promotion" box** should be ticked when a video contains an endorsement or sponsorship. UK guides say ticking it does not replace the labelling above.
- **Recommendations must be truthful.** See the next section.

### Saying we use tools we don't use: no
Claiming to use a product we don't use, to earn commission on it, is a misleading advertisement. That breaks UK advertising and consumer rules, it breaks the terms of most affiliate programmes (which can withhold earnings and close the account), and it puts the channel's credibility at risk for small sums. Claude won't write those claims.

It also wouldn't work well. People watching a business story are not shopping for video-making software, so those links would rarely convert.

### What to do instead
1. **Tools we really use.** If the channel adopts ElevenLabs for narration, its affiliate programme pays 22% of a referral's payments for the first year (from ElevenLabs' own announcement; check current terms when joining). A short, honest "how these videos are made" line can carry that link.
2. **Things that fit the audience:** books mentioned in a video (Amazon Associates or Audible), and business or money tools that relate to that video's topic and that you'd recommend anyway.
3. **Sponsorships later.** Once videos reliably get around 10,000 views, direct sponsors pay far more than affiliate links.

I couldn't confirm that Anthropic runs an affiliate programme for Claude.

### From day one?
One or two honest, labelled links from the first video are fine and cost nothing. Keep expectations low: affiliate income follows views, and at the start the only goal is getting people to watch.
